PART 1: BUSINESS OVERVIEW
1. Business concept
The business is an independent AI education company focused on helping people understand, use and build with artificial intelligence.

Its purpose is not simply to teach people how to use ChatGPT or Gemini.

It aims to teach people how to:
1. Understand artificial intelligence.
2. Communicate effectively with AI systems.
3. Solve real problems using AI.
4. Design automated workflows.
5. Connect different applications and services.
6. Build AI-powered automation systems.
7. Understand and design AI agents.
8. Apply these skills to education, business, agriculture and other industries.
Core message:
Learn AI. Apply AI. Build with AI.

The business will start in Rwanda, with the possibility of expanding to other African countries and eventually international markets.
2. Mission
Make practical AI education accessible to people who want to understand artificial intelligence and use it to solve real-world problems.
3. Vision
Become a trusted practical AI education provider that helps individuals and organizations develop useful AI, automation and problem-solving skills.
4. Target market
Primary audience
- Secondary school graduates.
- University students.
- TVET students.
- Recent graduates.
- Young people interested in technology.
- People who want to improve their productivity using AI.
Secondary audience
- Small business owners.
- Entrepreneurs.
- Teachers and educators.
- Content creators.

- Professionals.
- NGOs and organizations.
Geographic strategy
Stage	Market

5. The problem being addressed
Many people have access to AI tools but do not know how to use them effectively.

Some can ask ChatGPT questions, generate images or write content, but struggle when they need to:
- Create reliable outputs.
- Verify AI-generated information.
- Design reusable prompt systems.
- Automate repetitive tasks.
- Connect applications.
- Build AI-powered workflows.
- Understand AI agents.
- Apply AI to real business or educational problems.
The opportunity is to move learners beyond basic AI usage.
The educational gap
Level 1 — AI awareness

Learners know AI tools exist.

Level 2 — AI usage

Learners can use AI tools for everyday tasks.

Level 3 — Prompt engineering

Learners can communicate with AI more effectively.

Level 4 — Workflow thinking

Learners can identify and design repeatable processes.

Level 5 — Automation

Learners can connect applications and automate tasks.

Level 6 — AI-powered automation

Learners can integrate AI into automated processes.

Level 7 — AI agents and systems

Learners understand how to build and manage systems that use AI tools to perform multi-step tasks.

The business will help learners progress through these levels.
PART 2: EDUCATIONAL FRAMEWORK
6. The seven learning levels
These are the foundation of the educational business. They should guide all courses, lessons, projects and learning resources.
Level 1: AI Foundations
Learners understand what artificial intelligence is and how it works.

Topics:
- What is artificial intelligence?
- History and development of AI.
- Artificial intelligence vs machine learning.
- Machine learning vs deep learning.
- Generative AI.
- Large Language Models (LLMs).
- How AI generates responses.
- AI hallucinations.
- Bias and limitations.
- AI privacy and security.
- Responsible AI usage.
- Human judgment and AI.
Expected outcome:

Learners can explain AI concepts, identify AI limitations and use AI responsibly.
Level 2: Prompt Engineering
Learners understand how to communicate effectively with AI systems.

Topics:
- What is prompt engineering?
- Anatomy of a good prompt.
- Context and instructions.
- Defining roles and objectives.
- Constraints and output formats.
- Providing examples.
- Structured prompting.
- Advanced prompting techniques.
- Prompt chaining.
- Reusable prompt templates.
- Testing and evaluating prompts.
- Improving poor AI responses.
Expected outcome:

Learners can design, test, improve and reuse effective prompts.
Level 3: Workflow Thinking
Before automating something, learners must understand how the process works.

Topics:
- What is a workflow?
- Identifying repetitive tasks.
- Understanding inputs and outputs.
- Triggers and actions.
- Mapping business processes.
- Identifying bottlenecks.
- Decision points.
- Conditions and exceptions.
- Human involvement.
- Designing efficient processes.
Expected outcome:

Learners can identify a problem and describe how a workflow should operate before choosing an automation tool.
Level 4: No-Code Automation
Learners understand how to connect applications and automate repetitive processes.

Topics:
- Introduction to automation.
- Automation platforms.
- Triggers and actions.
- Connecting applications.
- Mapping data.
- Filters and conditions.
- Scheduling.
- Error handling.
- Testing automation.
- Monitoring workflows.
Expected outcome:

Learners can build basic automated workflows without needing advanced programming knowledge.
Level 5: AI-Powered Automation
Learners understand how AI can become part of an automated workflow.

Topics:
- Introduction to AI automation.
- Traditional automation vs AI automation.
- AI classification.
- Information extraction.
- Document summarization.
- Text generation.
- Translation.
- AI-powered customer inquiry processing.
- AI output evaluation.
- Human approval.
- Reliability and limitations.
Expected outcome:

Learners can design workflows where AI performs specific tasks within a larger automated process.
Level 6: AI Agents
Learners understand what AI agents are and how they operate.

Topics:
- What is an AI agent?
- AI agents vs chatbots.
- AI agents vs ordinary automation.
- Tools and integrations.
- Knowledge sources.
- Memory concepts.
- Multi-step task execution.
- Permissions and boundaries.
- Human approval.
- Agent evaluation.
- Monitoring and security.
Expected outcome:

Learners can explain AI agents and design a simple agent workflow with appropriate human oversight.
Level 7: AI Systems and Problem Solving
This level connects everything together.

Topics:
- Identifying real-world problems.
- Selecting appropriate AI solutions.
- System architecture.
- Combining AI, automation and software.
- Designing AI-powered products.
- Evaluating solutions.
- Cost and performance considerations.
- Security and privacy.
- Building prototypes.
- Testing and improving systems.
Expected outcome:

Learners can plan and demonstrate a practical AI-powered solution to a real problem.

Important: The first 28-day course introduces these levels. It does not promise mastery of all seven levels in four weeks.

Instead of MySQL tables, organize information into Firestore collections and documents.

Suggested collections:
Collection	Purpose

projectSubmissions	Learner projects

This is a starting data model, not a complete production schema.

Important Firestore design considerations:
- Use document IDs and references consistently.
- Avoid storing large lesson documents inside user profiles.
- Plan indexes for queries before launch.
- Avoid unbounded arrays in documents.
- Use pagination for large datasets.
- Use transactions or server-side logic for sensitive operations.
- Protect privileged fields through security rules and backend validation.
- Use server-side timestamps where appropriate.
24. Payment integration
Potential payment methods:
- MTN Mobile Money.
- Airtel Money.
- Other payment providers when commercially justified.
Required payment workflow:
1. Learner selects a course.
2. Platform creates a payment record.
3. Learner starts payment.
4. Payment provider processes the transaction.
5. Backend receives a callback or webhook.
6. Backend verifies the payment with the provider.
7. Backend checks whether the transaction has already been processed.
8. Backend marks the payment as verified.
9. Enrollment is created or activated.
10. Learner receives confirmation.
Critical requirements:
- Never trust a payment screenshot as proof.
- Never store mobile money PINs.
- Verify transactions server-side.
- Use idempotency to prevent duplicate enrollment.
- Maintain payment and refund records.
- Reconcile transactions with provider records.
- Do not give course access based only on a frontend success message.
25. Privacy and security
The platform will collect personal information and learner records.

It must consider Rwanda's Law No. 058/2021 relating to the protection of personal data and privacy.

Before collecting real learner information, investigate:
- Applicable data protection registration requirements.
- Privacy notices and consent.
- Data retention policies.
- User access and deletion rights.
- Security controls.
- Cross-border data transfers.
- Hosting and storage arrangements.
- Access permissions for administrators.
Use a qualified local adviser for compliance decisions.
Security requirements
- Firebase Authentication.
- Firestore Security Rules.
- Storage Security Rules.
- Role-based access control.
- Backend authorization checks.
- Input validation.
- Rate limiting.
- Audit logs.
- Secure secrets management.
- Backup and recovery planning.
- Protection against unauthorized certificate creation.
